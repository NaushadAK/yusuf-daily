Top story

Iran hits a US base in Bahrain as peace talks stall

Iran's Revolutionary Guards say six drones struck a US air-defence radar at a helicopter base in Bahrain on Sunday, their second claimed attack on US forces there in three days and the first after about two months of calm. Bahrain says it shot down several of the drones, and the US denies that the radar was destroyed. Talks between Washington and Tehran are stuck over Iran's conditions for reopening the Strait of Hormuz, where Iran also says two oil tankers hit mines and caught fire. The flare-up comes days before Trump, Xi and Putin are due to meet in Shenzhen on Thursday.

Something to think about

Electrons that jump where they "shouldn't"

In materials, electrons can only have certain energies, with forbidden "gaps" in between, a bit like a staircase where you can stand on steps but not between them. Researchers studying graphene, a sheet of carbon just one atom thick, have now caught electrons making jumps across these gaps that standard rules said should be very unlikely. Some of these transitions happened every single time.

Graphene is already being tested for faster electronics, better batteries and stronger, lighter composites. Learning exactly how its electrons move is what turns a strange lab result into a useful device.

Question for the day: Graphene is stronger than steel for its weight. If you could use a thin graphene layer anywhere on a car or a bike, where would you put it, and why?
