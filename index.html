Top story

Climate summit ends with no overall deal after two days of overtime

The COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Türkiye, closed on Sunday without agreement on their main text. Summit president Murat Kurum told the final session that the cover decision had not been adopted. The European Union and others rejected the draft as too weak because it did not say the world must move away from fossil fuels, while big oil producers such as Saudi Arabia had pushed to keep that wording out. The draft did restate the 1.5°C warming limit, and some smaller decisions were still agreed. The talks ran nearly 48 hours past their Friday deadline.

Something to think about

Why almost 200 countries could not agree

UN climate summits work by consensus: a decision passes only if no country formally objects. That means one group can block a text the rest are ready to accept. In Antalya, some countries said the draft was too weak, while others refused stronger wording on fossil fuels, so the main deal fell through.

Consensus protects small and poor countries from being outvoted, but it also makes big changes slow. Engineers face a similar choice when designing safety systems: should a machine stop if any one sensor raises an alarm, or only if most of them agree? The first is safer but can stop for false alarms; the second runs more smoothly but might miss a real problem.

Question for the day: Should world climate decisions need every country's agreement, or would a large majority be fairer? What would you lose either way?
