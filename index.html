Israel's president is set to name who gets the first try at forming a government

After two days of talks with parties, Gadi Eisenkot, who leads the opposition bloc against Prime Minister Netanyahu, was recommended by 67 of the 120 Knesset members, against 46 for Netanyahu. President Isaac Herzog said he would decide today, but warned that it is unclear whether Eisenkot can actually assemble a government because parties refuse to work together. The chosen candidate gets 28 days, which can be extended by 14. Check the news for Herzog's final announcement.

Something to think about

How does a diya's oil climb up the wick?

Today is Diwali, and every lamp lit tonight relies on a quiet bit of physics. Oil rises through the tiny gaps between the cotton fibres of the wick, against gravity, because the oil clings to the fibres and is pulled upwards. This is called capillary action.

The same effect moves water up plant stems and through paper towels, and engineers use it in heat pipes that cool electronics.

Question for the day: would a thicker wick or a thinner one raise oil faster, and how could you test it with a few strips of cloth and a glass of coloured water?
