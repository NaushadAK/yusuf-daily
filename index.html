Top story

Jensen Huang resigns as Nvidia's chief executive

Jensen Huang, who co-founded Nvidia and led it for more than three decades, resigned on Thursday as president, chief executive and board member, with immediate effect, according to the company's filing with US regulators. Finance chief Colette Kress becomes interim CEO while the board searches for a permanent successor, and Harvey Jones becomes the company's first board chair. His exit follows reports about his personal conduct, including a relationship that US officials link to Chinese intelligence; China denies the claim. Nvidia's shares closed about 4% lower, and members of the US Congress want him to answer questions.

Something to think about

A giant new camera finds two hidden neighbours of our galaxy

The Vera C. Rubin Observatory in Chile has found two small, faint galaxies orbiting our own Milky Way that nobody had seen before. It found them using early data covering only about 7% of the sky, so astronomers think hundreds more may be out there.

The observatory's secret is its camera, the largest digital camera ever built, with about 3,200 megapixels. That is roughly 270 times the pixels of a good phone camera. The camera has to be cooled far below freezing so the sensors don't add noise, and a huge mirror steers faint light onto them. Building it took mechanical, thermal and optical engineers working together for years.

Question for the day: Rubin will photograph the whole visible sky every few nights for ten years. If each photo is billions of pixels, how would you design a system to spot the one tiny dot that moved or got brighter between two nights?
