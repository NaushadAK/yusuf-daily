Top story

Quad leaders meet in Manila for the first time in over two years

Prime Minister Modi, US President Trump, Japan's Prime Minister Sanae Takaichi and Australia's Prime Minister Anthony Albanese met in Manila yesterday on the sidelines of the ASEAN summits. In a joint statement they opposed any attempt to change the status quo in the region by force or pressure, and condemned terrorism in all its forms, including cross-border terrorism. They agreed on the group's first joint maritime surveillance exercise, planned for March 2027 near Guam, extended their undersea-cable cooperation to energy cables, and launched a Quad Business Leaders' Forum that India will host in 2027.

Something to think about

Shooting stars from a comet's dusty trail

The Leonid meteor shower peaked in the early hours of this morning. Every November, Earth passes through the trail of dust left by Comet Tempel-Tuttle. The grains, most no bigger than a grain of sand, hit our atmosphere at about 71 km per second, and the air in front of them gets so hot that it glows as a streak of light. This year's display is modest, about 10 to 15 meteors an hour at best, but in 1966 observers saw thousands a minute.

Engineers face the same physics when a spacecraft comes home: at those speeds, squeezed air heats up enormously, which is why capsules need heat shields that slowly burn away to protect what is inside.

Question for the day: A tiny grain of dust burns up, but a spacecraft survives re-entry. What design choices, in shape and materials, do you think make the difference?
