Top story

Wall Street suffers its worst week in years as the AI sell-off deepens

US shares fell sharply again on Friday. The Nasdaq dropped 5.28% to 22,617.73, wiping out all of its gains for 2026, while the S&P 500 lost 3.70% to 6,910.39 and the Dow fell 949.73 points (1.93%) to 48,147.33. Both the S&P 500 and the Dow are now in a "correction", meaning more than 10% below their recent peaks; the S&P 500 fell 8.6% over the week. Chipmakers led the fall, with Nvidia down about 14%, as investors grew more doubtful about how much money AI will really earn. Indian markets are closed for the weekend, so the effect here will show on Monday.

World in brief 05

  • Shenzhen talks end with a short four-point declaration

    After their first three-way meeting, Trump, Xi and Putin issued a joint statement on peace covering UN reform, energy supply, the International Criminal Court and nuclear risk. They repeated that "a nuclear war cannot be won and must never be fought" and promised talks on reducing nuclear dangers.

  • Ukraine and Iran left out

    The declaration made no mention of the war in Ukraine or the conflict involving Iran, the two issues many expected to dominate. Kyiv criticised the leaders for discussing its future without Ukraine in the room.

  • Russia warns Estonia over border closure

    Moscow accused Estonia of putting up a new "Iron Curtain" and promised a response. Estonia plans to close its road crossings with Russia from 1 December after blaming Russian intelligence for a foiled arson attack on a drone maker.

  • Climate summit runs into its deadline

    The COP31 talks were due to end on Friday with countries still deeply divided. Climate groups criticised a shorter draft text that reportedly no longer mentions fossil fuels; earlier drafts had called for a faster move away from them.

  • Attacks continue in Ukraine

    Russian strikes hit bridges across the Dnipro in Kyiv and disrupted heating and water in parts of the capital this week, while Ukraine kept up its attacks on the Kerch Bridge to Crimea. No ceasefire is in sight.

India 05

  • Cyclone Akvan crosses the coast near Gopalpur

    Akvan made landfall on the north Andhra–south Odisha coast on Friday morning and weakened into a deep depression. Odisha reported no major damage or loss of life after evacuating more than three lakh people; Patrapur in Ganjam district received 209 mm of rain, and many trains were cancelled.

  • India's first home-grown bird flu vaccine for poultry

    Agriculture Minister Shivraj Singh Chouhan launched an H5N1 vaccine for chickens developed by ICAR's animal disease lab in Bhopal. A single shot is expected to protect birds for about six months; it is for poultry, not people.

  • Supreme Court clears bike taxis in Karnataka

    The court dismissed the Karnataka government's appeal and upheld the High Court ruling that allows bike taxi services to run in the state.

  • Congress ahead in Rajasthan panchayat polls

    Early results from the rural local body elections showed Congress leading the ruling BJP in both panchayat samiti and zila parishad seats.

  • PM attends first SCO Civilisational Dialogue Forum

    Prime Minister Modi attended the forum in Delhi. The presence of a Pakistani minister drew criticism, with BJP leaders saying dialogue cannot go on alongside terrorism.

Kerala 05

  • Quarry blasting blamed for Idukki landslide

    A district disaster authority report says rock blasting that went on until 11 pm triggered the 14 November landslide near Kattappana that killed a farmer, even though there had been no heavy rain. It recommends an inquiry into officials who failed to act against illegal quarrying.

  • Kerala's city air is getting worse

    A CREA study found fine-dust (PM2.5) levels in Kerala's cities are within national limits but above WHO guidelines, and higher than in Tamil Nadu, Karnataka and Puducherry.

  • "Operation Paithal" to protect young fish

    The Fisheries Department began a statewide drive to stop the catching and sale of undersized fish, to protect stocks for the future.

  • A new vegetable mission from next year

    Agriculture Minister T Siddique said the state will launch a vegetable mission from the next financial year to boost home-grown produce and cut heavy pesticide use.

  • Anti-narcotics squads to get pistol training

    Following a rise in attacks on officers, police chiefs have been told to arrange weapons training for anti-narcotics squad members.

Something to think about

Building blocks for the Moon, printed from moon dust

A student and a mechanical engineer at the University of Arkansas have 3D-printed small interlocking blocks, shaped like crosses and the letter H, from material that imitates lunar soil. They used a laser to melt and fuse the powder layer by layer, so no glue or cement is needed. That matters because every kilogram of binder would otherwise have to be flown from Earth.

The pieces are only a few centimetres across and still need to be made stronger. The team also has to make sure they fit together even when each print comes out slightly different in size, a real-world problem engineers call tolerance.

Question for the day: If you could only use what is already on the Moon, what would you build first, and how would you join the parts together without any bolts or glue?
