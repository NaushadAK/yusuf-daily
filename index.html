Top story

Climate summit runs into overtime with a weaker deal on the table

The COP31 climate talks in Antalya, Türkiye, missed their Friday deadline and went on through Saturday night. Summit president Murat Kurum put forward a final text, which he calls the Antalya Declaration, and said he believes every country can accept it. To get there, the draft dropped all mention of moving away from fossil fuels and a proposed $120 billion goal for helping countries adapt to climate change. Countries had until 8:30 am IST today to object, and a session to approve the deal is due to begin at 11:30 am IST. Climate groups say the text is far weaker than earlier drafts.

Something to think about

Why a Test match lead of 261 still might not be enough

India's record score in Wellington looks huge, but a Test match has a fixed amount of time: five days of about 90 overs each. Every over India spend batting is an over they cannot use to bowl New Zealand out. That is why captains "declare", stopping their innings early on purpose, even with batters still in.

It is a classic trade-off, the same kind engineers face all the time: more strength often means more weight, and more safety often means more cost. The best choice is rarely the extreme one; it is the balance point where the gain from a bit more of one thing equals what you lose of the other.

Question for the day: If you were India's captain this morning, when would you declare, and how would you decide? Think of one machine or design you know where engineers had to make a similar trade-off.
